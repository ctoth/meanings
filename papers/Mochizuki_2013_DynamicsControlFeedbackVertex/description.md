Proves that the feedback vertex set (FVS) of a regulatory network's directed dependency graph is a set of "determining nodes": observing the time courses of just the FVS variables asymptotically determines the trajectory of every variable in the network, and the FVS is computable from the wiring diagram alone — independent of the (unknown) nonlinear rate laws. Demonstrates a 113-molecule EGF signaling network monitored by 5 molecules, a 16-gene ascidian developmental network monitored by 1, and a 21-component mammalian circadian model monitored by 7, with ~99% in silico identification rates, and shows that clamping the FVS onto a target attractor steers the whole system (the observational/monitor companion to Part I's control result). Directly relevant to treating a dictionary as a definition digraph: the FVS is the minimal "grounding set" of words whose fixed meanings force the rest by following the now-acyclic residual — depending only on which words appear in which definitions, not on semantic content.
