## 0. One-paragraph executive summary

A non-negative irreducible matrix has, by Perron–Frobenius, a unique positive dominant eigenvector — "the self-consistent valuation the relational structure implies about itself." That object has been independently rediscovered, with near-empty cross-citation, in at least six fields. **For this repo the headline test failed in an instructive way:** PageRank and the un-damped Perron eigenvector, run on the OEWN definition digraph the repo already analyses, do **not** reproduce the combinatorial grounding-kernel ranking. PageRank with the repo's edge convention (`u → v` = "u occurs in the definition of v") ranks **sink-like leaf words highest** (`magnificat`, `palaquium_gutta`, `niobe`, `laocoon`) and ranks the combinatorial seed hubs (`small`, `large`, `white`, `can`, `plant`, `body`, `water`) in the **bottom decile** (global PageRank ranks 60 000–155 000 of 160 010). The un-damped Perron eigenvector on the strongly-connected kernel core (8 138 nodes, dominant eigenvalue λ\* ≈ 3.744) ranks high the **densest local cycle clusters** (`potassium` / `alkali_metal` / `rubidium`; `vasopressin` / `vasoconstrictor` / `angiotensin`; `gastrula` / `gastrulation`) — chemistry and anatomy micro-loops — not foundational vocabulary. So: the "canonical soft grounding vocabulary" claim, *in its naive form*, is **falsified on OEWN**. What survives — and is the actual paper — is (a) the convergent-rediscovery history, which is real and well-sourced, and (b) the precise diagnosis of *why* the naive spectral anchor fails here and what the correct spectral object is (reverse-graph / hub-side PageRank on the **condensation DAG of the kernel**, not authority-side PageRank on the raw digraph), plus the Frobenius-normal-form layering result.

**The one-line claim the table licenses:** every field that studies a *constitutive relational system with no external units* — production flows, lexical co-occurrence, hyperlink/citation structure, social influence, Markovian dynamics, mutation–selection — has independently arrived at "take the dominant eigenvector of the non-negative system matrix" as the way the system names its own canonical anchor. The damping term in PageRank, the teleportation in EigenTrust, the "augmented" technology matrix in Sraffa, and (in this repo) the Kernel/Rest decomposition are all the *same move*: surgery to restore the irreducibility that Perron–Frobenius requires.

## 2. Citation-disjointness measurements

What I could verify from the literature (sources at the end). Quantification is coarse — "near-empty", "partial" — because I did not run a systematic forward-citation crawl; the qualitative pattern is well documented.

### 2a. Economics ↔ mathematics: **near-empty, and famously so.**
Bidard, Erreygers & Parys, "All but one: How pioneers of linear economics overlooked Perron–Frobenius mathematics" (2013, Univ. Antwerp WP 2013-030) documents that **Charasoff (1910s), Bray (1922), Remak (1929), Leontief (1930s), and Sraffa (1928→1960) all built linear-production models without ever invoking Perron–Frobenius** — even though the theorem was published 1907–1912, before most of their work. The *single* exception was the French Jesuit mathematician **Maurice Potron**, who used Perron–Frobenius in the core of an input–output model **as early as 1911** — and was almost completely ignored by economists for ~90 years. Sraffa explicitly sought mathematical help from non-economists (Besicovitch, Watson) and the eigenvector reading of the standard commodity was supplied *after the fact* by Newman (1962) and Pasinetti. So the econ↔math bridge is the textbook case of disciplinary disjointness.

### 2b. Economics ↔ NLP/IR: **empty** (to the limit of what I can verify).
I found no evidence that the LSA / distributional-semantics literature (Deerwester et al. 1990; Landauer & Dumais 1997; and successors) cites Sraffa, Leontief, Potron, or input–output economics. The connection — LSA's leading semantic axis is a Perron eigenvector of the co-occurrence Gram matrix, just as Sraffa's standard commodity is a Perron eigenvector of the technology matrix — appears nowhere I can find. This is the cleanest open gap.

### 2c. Web search ↔ bibliometrics ↔ sociology: **partial, and mostly retroactive.**
Brin & Page's original 1998 paper ("The anatomy of a large-scale hypertextual Web search engine") cites **Garfield (citation indexing), Marchiori (HyperSearch), and Kleinberg (HITS)** — i.e. it cites the *web/IR-adjacent* lineage. It does **not** cite Hubbell (1965), Pinski & Narin (1976), Katz (1953), or Bonacich. Hubbell's 1965 *Sociometry* paper is, in retrospect, "PageRank for citation/sociometric networks" 33 years early, and Pinski & Narin 1976 is eigenvector journal prestige — yet neither was on PageRank's reference list. The bibliometrics↔web-search bridge was built *afterward*, by historians and review writers (e.g. Langville & Meyer's *Google's PageRank and Beyond* surveys the lineage; later "PageRank has been reinvented many times" commentary names Landau 1895, Pinski–Narin 1976, Hubbell 1965). Within sociology itself, eigenvector centrality was *itself* a rediscovery: Landau (1895, chess tournaments) → Wei → Kendall (sport ranking) → Berge → Bonacich (1972/1987). So even the "single field" has internal disjointness.

### 2d. Symbol-grounding / dictionary-graph literature ↔ any of the above: **empty.**
Nothing in Massé 2008, Picard 2013, Vincent-Lamarre 2014, Levary 2012, or Harnad 1990 (per the repo's notes and synthesis) cites PageRank, eigenvector centrality, Sraffa, Markov stationary distributions, or input–output analysis. The dictionary-kernel program picks its grounding anchor **combinatorially** (minimum feedback vertex set — discrete, NP-hard, many tied optima) and has, as far as I can tell, never connected that to the **spectral** anchor-selection lineage. *That non-connection is the Swanson link this prompt set is built around.* The honest finding (see §5): when you actually run the spectral method here, it does not agree with the combinatorial one — so the link, while real as history, does not yield the hoped-for "the spectral method dissolves the which-MinSet problem" payoff on OEWN.

**Bridges that DO exist:** econ↔math (negative, but extensively written up); bibliometrics↔web-search (partial, retroactive, via Langville & Meyer and history-of-science commentary); within-sociology rediscovery chain (documented). **Bridges that DON'T:** econ↔NLP; econ↔web-search; dictionary-grounding↔anything spectral; NLP-LSA↔sociology-eigenvector-centrality.

## 4. The reconciliation: Perron–Frobenius on the Kernel, recursion on the Rest

### 4a. Where the spectral method is *licensed*
Perron–Frobenius needs `M` irreducible = the digraph strongly connected. The OEWN definition digraph is **not** strongly connected — but the repo's **Kernel** is precisely the maximal sub-digraph in which every node still has an outgoing non-self edge after peeling, and the **Core** = union of source SCCs inside the Kernel. The 8 138-node giant SCC *is* an irreducible block. So the principled pipeline is:
1. Peel to the Kernel (the repo's `compute_kernel`). The **Rest** (141 859 nodes, post the self-loop fix; was 147,157) is a reducible DAG-with-pendant-cycles handled by the recursive layer-unrolling (`compute_layer_map`) the repo already does. *No spectral method should touch the Rest* — there is no unique Perron eigenvector there.
2. On the Kernel, take the **condensation** (DAG of SCCs). This is the **Frobenius normal form**: a block-triangular reordering where the diagonal blocks are the SCCs. The single giant block (8 138) sits at the "bottom" (it is reached from many other tiny cyclic blocks and from the Rest); the 508 source SCCs sit at the "top" of the kernel condensation (nothing inside the kernel points into them) — that's the **Core**.
3. Within the giant block, Perron–Frobenius gives a unique positive `v*` (we computed it: λ\* ≈ 3.744). **But §3b shows `v*` localises on dense technical micro-cliques, not on grounding vocabulary** — so the within-block Perron eigenvector is *not* "the canonical MinSet weighting." It is the wrong functional for the grounding question, even though it is the unique anchor of *that block*.
4. The block-triangular structure *does* give a principled layering: SCC-condensation topological depth = a Core-vs-deeper-Kernel ordering. The Core (source SCCs, depth 0 in the condensation) is "most upstream"; the giant SCC is downstream of it. This **matches** the Picard/Vincent-Lamarre Core-vs-Satellite split by construction (Core = source SCCs is *defined* that way in `graph_analysis.source_sccs`). So the Frobenius normal form *reconstructs* the existing layering rather than adding a new one — a consistency check, not a new result.

### 4b. When the FVS anchor and the spectral anchor agree — and when they don't
- **Disagree (the OEWN finding):** authority-side PageRank ↔ feedback-vertex-set seed. Recall < 10 % at the top; the seed hubs are in PageRank's *bottom* decile. Reason: the FVS heuristic rewards **high total degree / high out-degree** ("this word is in many definitions, cut it"); authority-side PageRank rewards **being pointed at by important nodes**, which on this digraph means **being a definitional sink**. Opposite ends of the flow.
- **Plausibly agree (untested prediction, §3g):** **reverse**-PageRank ↔ feedback-vertex-set seed. Both are "out-flow" measures; `choose_feedback_vertex`'s key is literally `internal_out + internal_in`, and reverse-PageRank is the eigenvector relaxation of exactly that. **The cleanest single experiment to settle the whole Swanson link is to compute reverse-PageRank on the kernel and measure its Spearman ρ with the seed's degree-score ordering. Prediction: ρ > 0.6.**
- **Where the FVS picture is *intrinsically* better:** the FVS gives a *set* (a basis you actually instantiate); the eigenvector gives a *ranking* (you still have to threshold). The eigenvector dissolves the "which MinSet" tie only if you also fix a threshold rule — which re-introduces an arbitrary choice. So the "Sraffa : numéraire :: Perron eigenvector : MinSet" analogy is *looser* than the prompt hoped: Sraffa's standard commodity is canonical because it needs no threshold (it's the whole eigenvector as a composite good); a grounding *vocabulary* does need a cardinality, so the spectral version trades "which set among ties" for "which cutoff" — not obviously a win.

### 4c. Reducibility *within* the Kernel
The Kernel has 9 139 SCCs, 508 of them sources (post the self-loop fix; pre-fix 3,841 / 286). Perron–Frobenius applies *per SCC*; there is no single Perron eigenvector for the whole (reducible) Kernel. The standard fix (PageRank's damping; EigenTrust's teleport; Sraffa's "augmentation") is to add a small uniform perturbation making the whole thing irreducible — at the cost of the result being partly an artefact of the perturbation size `1−d`. The repo's Kernel/Core decomposition is the **structural alternative to damping**: instead of smearing the graph to force one eigenvector, it tells you the block structure and lets you take a genuine Perron eigenvector *per block*. That is the actual conceptual contribution available here, and it generalises: *the Kernel/Rest split is the licence boundary for spectral anchor-selection on any reducible relational system.*

## 5. Falsifiers / scope — and what actually got falsified

**(a) Falsified on OEWN: "the Perron eigenvector of the definition graph is a canonical soft grounding vocabulary."** Authority-side PageRank ranks definitional *sinks* highest and the combinatorial backbone in the bottom decile (§3c). Un-damped Perron on the SCC localises on technical micro-cliques (§3b). Neither is "how foundational is this word." The claim survives only in the *reversed* form (hub-side / reverse-PageRank), which is **predicted** to work but **was not run** here — that prediction is the falsifiable residue.

**(b) "It adds nothing over raw degree/frequency":** *partially true.* Within the SCC, un-damped Perron ρ ≈ 0.78 with internal in-degree — mostly a re-skin. Globally, PageRank ρ ≈ 0.40 with in-degree — not *just* degree, but its departures from degree (sink-localisation) make it *worse*, not better, as a grounding signal. So the "adds value" bar is not cleared.

**(c) "Same matrix algebra ⇒ same idea?" — Cinderella-slipper assessment:** *honest answer: it's a real family resemblance, not an identity.* What flows differs essentially — *value* (Sraffa, conserved in exchange), *probability* (Markov, conserved, sums to 1), *influence/authority* (PageRank/Bonacich, not conserved — that's why damping is needed), *co-occurrence mass* (LSA, symmetric, no direction), *fitness-weighted population* (Eigen). The *unifying* invariant is narrower than "same idea": it is "**a relational system with no external units has, when irreducible, a unique scale-free self-consistent weighting, and every field that needed one found it the same way (or had to surgically restore irreducibility to get one)**." That is true and non-trivial. "It's all PageRank" overclaims; "it's all the Perron eigenvector of the system matrix, modulo an irreducibility fix" is exactly right.

**(d) Perron vs Fiedler — the differences-not-anchors reading:** the *Perron* eigenvector (dominant, all-positive) is the "anchor"; the *Fiedler* vector (second-smallest of the Laplacian, sign-changing) is the "cleavage" — spectral bisection, the structuralist "meaning = system of differences" picture. The repo currently computes neither, but the Fiedler / spectral-clustering direction is a **genuinely separate paper** ("Saussure's *valeur* is the Fiedler vector": meaning lives in the *second* eigenvector, not the first) and should not be conflated with this one. Worth flagging as a sibling.

**(e) Scope of the rediscovery-history claim:** that part is robust and well-sourced (Bidard–Erreygers–Parys 2013 for econ↔math; Langville & Meyer 2006 and the standard "PageRank reinvented" commentary for bibliometrics↔web-search; the Landau→Wei→Kendall→Berge→Bonacich chain for within-sociology). The OEWN-specific payoff is the part that did not survive contact with the data.

## 6. The proposed paper(s)

**Two papers, because the surviving content splits cleanly.**

### Paper A (history & philosophy of science venue — e.g. *Synthese*, *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science*, or a network-science generalist outlet like *J. Complex Networks* / *Network Science*)
- **Title:** *"The Self-Naming System: Perron–Frobenius Valuation as a Convergent Rediscovery Across Economics, Linguistics, Web Search, and Sociology."*
- **Thesis:** Every field that studies a constitutive relational system with no external units independently arrived at "the dominant eigenvector of the non-negative system matrix" as the system's canonical anchor — and (with one exception, Potron 1911) did so without cross-citation; the irreducibility-restoring tricks (PageRank's damping, Sraffa's augmentation, EigenTrust's teleport) are the *same move* re-invented.
- **The one figure:** the rediscovery table of §1 rendered as a timeline-with-citation-edges, edges drawn only where one rediscovery actually cited another — the visual punchline being how few edges there are.

### Paper B (applied-math / network-science / computational-linguistics venue — e.g. *LREC/COLING*, *Comput. Linguist.*, or *J. Complex Networks*)
- **Title:** *"The Wrong Eigenvector: Why PageRank Does Not Recover the Grounding Kernel of a Dictionary, and Which Eigenvector Does."*
- **Thesis:** On the OEWN definition digraph, authority-side PageRank and the un-damped Perron eigenvector of the strongly-connected core fail to recover the combinatorial grounding kernel (numbers from §3); the failure is diagnostic — the combinatorial feedback-vertex seed is an *out-flow* object, so the matching spectral object is **reverse-PageRank on the Kernel**, not PageRank on the digraph; and the Kernel/Rest decomposition is precisely the structural alternative to damping (it tells you per-block where Perron–Frobenius is licensed). Includes the Frobenius-normal-form ↔ Core/Satellite layering correspondence as a consistency result.
