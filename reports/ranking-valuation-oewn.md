# Ranking-based-semantics valuation of the OEWN definition digraph — does a gradual semantics beat `log(out-degree)`?

**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Type:** computation + verdict on connection #6 of `reports/sibling-tools-connection.md`
**Reproduce:** `uv run python scripts/ranking_valuation_oewn.py` → writes `reports/ranking-valuation-oewn.json` (partial JSON is rewritten after every scope, so a kill keeps results) and a console summary; run log in `reports/ranking-valuation-oewn.run.log`. Set `RV_SKIP_FULL=1` to skip the (slow) full-graph pass. A pickle of the graph + kernel analysis is cached to `scratch/ranking_valuation_graph_cache.pkl` so reruns skip the ~90 s `wn` build. Module: the script reuses `meanings.{wordnet_pipeline,graph_analysis,annotations}` and `argumentation.ranking` (h-categoriser, categoriser, burden, counting); no new code in `src/meanings/argumentation_bridge.py` was needed.

This executes "Independent Contribution / next step #3" from `reports/sibling-tools-connection.md` §6: the spectral pass (`reports/spectral-valuation-oewn.md`) found reverse-PageRank ≈ out-degree and "adds little over degree"; `argumentation/ranking.py` has gradual / ranking-based semantics *designed* to rank arguments by defensibility accounting for attack topology rather than degree count. The question: **does any of them add real signal over `log(out-degree)` for predicting the psycholinguistic norms?** — answered with the incremental-R²-over-degree number, exactly as the spectral report did for reverse-PageRank.

---

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**No. Another principled score that also collapses to degree — a publishable negative result, twin to the reverse-PageRank one.** Over the 9 007 kernel nodes with all three psycholinguistic norms, the best incremental R² of *any* ranking-semantics score over `log(out-degree)` is **+0.023** (burden, concreteness) — and that number is an artefact of the baseline being ≈ 0 there (`log(out-degree)` predicts concreteness with R² ≈ 0.0001; concreteness is precisely the norm degree fails at), so +0.023 of trivially-small absolute R². For the norms where `log(out-degree)` carries real signal — frequency (R² ≈ 0.15) and age-of-acquisition (R² ≈ 0.065) — every ranking score adds **+0.0001 to +0.005**. The mechanism is mechanical: on the *attack* reading of the definition digraph, `argumentation.ranking`'s categoriser/burden/counting semantics are arithmetic re-skins of degree — categoriser/burden/counting in the **forward** ("definability") orientation correlate with **in-degree** at ρ ≈ ±0.95 (they are essentially `1/(1+in-degree)`); in the **reverse** ("foundational productivity") orientation they correlate with **out-degree** at ρ ≈ −0.59 to −0.66. Worse, the *sign is wrong for "foundational"*: a heavily-used genus word like `act::n` has *many* attackers in the reverse orientation, so it gets a *low* gradual score — these semantics rank dictionary *leaves* high and *hubs* low. h-categoriser's top tier is even coarser: every unattacked node gets exactly 1.0, so its "top 20" is just the first 20 nodes alphabetically (`about::a, absence::n, accepting::a, …`). None of these rankings recovers the abstract-genus vocabulary (`act, degree, time, part, …`) that reverse-PageRank put at the top; none correlates meaningfully with seed/MinSet membership (ρ ≈ ±0.03–0.14), the Kernel layer index (ρ ≈ ±0.1), or even the FVS heuristic's own `internal_out + internal_in` key (ρ ≈ −0.35 to −0.65 — *anti*-correlated; overlap@500 with the FVS top is ≈ 0.006 forward, **0.0** reverse). The honest framing: **`argumentation.ranking`'s gradual semantics, applied to a definition digraph read as an attack framework, are a complicated way of recomputing `1/(1+degree)` — they answer "how few words is this word defined in terms of" / "how few glosses does this word appear in", which is degree by another name and the *opposite* of foundationalness; they do not give a non-degenerate grounding-strength score.**

## 1. Set-up

### 1a. Graph / kernel baseline (this run, `oewn:2024`)
- Nodes (`lemma::pos`): **160 010** · directed edges: **677 823** (`u → v` = "u occurs in the gloss of v").
- Kernel (leaf-stripped): **18 151** · Core (`source-union`): **510** · Satellites: **17 641** · combinatorial seed (`exact-small-greedy` MinSet): **5 044** · residual cyclic SCCs after the seed: **0** (so the layer index is fully defined on the kernel). — These are the **post-self-loop-fix** numbers (commit 7d12e64), larger than the spectral report's older `12 853 / 288 / 2 370`.
- Psycholinguistic join (lemma part of each node, via `meanings.annotations.load_annotation_csvs` on `data/psycholinguistic/{frequency,age_of_acquisition,concreteness}.csv` — SUBTLEX-US Zipf / Kuperman et al. AoA / Brysbaert et al. concreteness): **27 356** nodes with all three norms (of 160 010); **9 007** of those are in the kernel.

### 1b. The two orientations (mirrors the spectral report's forward/reverse split)
`argumentation.ranking`'s semantics all read an `ArgumentationFramework`'s defeat relation and build an *attackers* index. Edge `u → v` in the meanings digraph means "u occurs in v's gloss", so:
- **forward** — Dung AF with `defeats = {(u,v)}` ("u attacks v"). Then `attackers[v]` = the words occurring in v's gloss = **in-neighbours**. A gradual semantics here rewards words with *few / weak definiens*: "how self-contained is this word's definition" — the **in-degree** side. (Called *"definability"* in the script.)
- **reverse** — Dung AF on the *transpose*: `defeats = {(v,u)}` so `attackers[u]` = the words u occurs in = **out-neighbours**. A gradual semantics here rewards words used in *few / weak* glosses — the **out-degree** side, the orientation reverse-PageRank used. (Called *"foundational productivity"* — though see §0: the *sign* comes out backwards for "foundational".)

### 1c. Semantics run (`argumentation/ranking.py`, the public symbols)
`h_categoriser_ranking` (`score = 1/(1 + min(1, Σ score(attacker)))`), `categoriser_scores` (`= 1/(1 + Σ score(attacker))`, uncapped), `burden_numbers` (`Bur_i = 1 + Σ 1/Bur_{i−1}(attacker)`, lower = more acceptable, 30 fixed iterations), `counting_ranking` (damped `1/(1 + d·Σ score(attacker))`, d = 0.98). Scopes: **kernel subgraph** (18 151 nodes / 73 654 edges — run to convergence; h-categoriser converges in 22 iterations, categoriser/counting in 35–42, all `converged=True`) and the **full digraph** (160 010 / 677 823 — iteration-capped at 25 because pure-Python iteration over 678 k edges to tol 1e-9 is impractical; the *order* is already stable at that cap even where absolute scores are not; the per-block `converged` flag in the JSON records this). `argumentation`'s `discussion_based_ranking` / `tuples_ranking` / `iterated_graded_ranking` were not run — they are O(depth · graph) path-counters whose answer on a sparse 678 k-edge digraph is again a degree count and which are even slower in pure Python; the four above are the contraction-style ("h-categorizer family") ones the task named.

This is **the first twenty nodes in alphabetical order** that land in the top tier — i.e. the leaf-ish words whose definiens are themselves all top-tier, so the fixpoint pins them at exactly 1.0 (h-cat) or the same tied value (categoriser/burden/counting). The ranking has *no resolution at the top*: it can tell you "this word is definitionally near a leaf" but not *which* near-leaf word matters. (The FVS heuristic's own top — `act, part, body, can, small, form, water, large, …` — appears nowhere; overlap@500 ≈ 0.006.)
- **reverse h_categoriser (kernel):** also a flat alphabetical top tier — `aardvark::n, abbess::n, abbot::n, aberration::n, abeyance::n, abolitionist::n, …` — the words that occur in *no* gloss (out-degree 0, so zero attackers in the reverse orientation), again pinned at 1.0. Pure definitional sinks.
- **reverse categoriser / burden / counting (kernel):** these *do* break the tie (the uncapped/damped aggregation distinguishes "1 weak attacker" from "0 attackers") — top-20 = `purposefully::r, uniformity::n, airtight::a, orthographic::a, phonological::a, complexion::n, skinned::a, chromatic_color::n, shakespeare::n, theatrical_producer::n, give_rise::v, legal_tender::n, affective::a, beheaded::a, utopia::n, recreational::a, batsman::n, outer_boundary::n, antioxidant::n, body_covering::n`. These are words that appear in *one or two* other glosses — peripheral, not the abstract-genus vocabulary. **This is the wrong end of the ranking for "foundational":** `act::n`, which appears in hundreds of glosses, is buried near the *bottom* of every reverse-orientation ranking, because in the reverse orientation it has hundreds of attackers. To use these semantics as a foundationalness score you would have to read them *inverted* — and inverted, `burden` reverse is just `+out-degree` (ρ = +0.659), i.e. degree again, with all the long-tail noise the spectral report described.

## 4. Why this happens (and why it was foreseeable)

The spectral report's diagnosis applies verbatim, one level up. A gradual / ranking-based semantics in the h-categorizer family is, by construction, **a fixpoint of a monotone function of the multiset of attacker scores**. On a sparse digraph where most nodes have either zero attackers (pinned at the max value) or a handful (so the fixpoint is dominated by the *count* of attackers, the attacker scores being all ≈ the same), the fixpoint is a smooth invertible function of in-degree (forward) or out-degree (reverse). It cannot be otherwise: the only structural information a gradual semantics extracts beyond "how many things attack you" is "and how strong are *they*" — and on this graph the attackers of a typical node are themselves typical nodes with typical scores, so that second-order term washes out. The categoriser family was designed for *small, hand-curated* argument graphs where attack strength genuinely varies; on a 678 k-edge definitional digraph it degenerates to degree, exactly as PageRank's eigenvector did. **"Another principled score that also collapses to degree" — the negative-result outcome the task explicitly flagged as publishable — is what we got.** And the *direction* problem is worse than the spectral report's: reverse-PageRank at least put the right words on top (the genus hubs) even if its rank order was mostly degree; the attack-reading gradual semantics put the *wrong* words on top (leaves), because "many attackers ⇒ low score" is the opposite of "used in many definitions ⇒ foundational". Reading a definition edge as *attack* is the original sin (the bridge module's docstring already says definition edges are more like *support* than attack); a *support*-aware gradual semantics — Potyka's `gradual.py` over a weighted bipolar AF, or a QBAF where definition edges add strength — would at least have the sign right, but on this graph it would still be degree-dominated for the same fixpoint reason. There is no free lunch here: the structure that would make a gradual semantics non-degenerate (heterogeneous edge weights, real conflict) is exactly the structure the `lemma::pos` projection threw away (polysemy, prototype exceptions — see `reports/sibling-tools-connection.md` §7).

## 5. What this means for the "Discrete Grounding and Spectral Valuation" paper

1. **Report it as the negative result it is, alongside reverse-PageRank.** The paper's "is there a continuous foundationalness score better than degree?" section now has *two* failed candidates with the same diagnosis: reverse-PageRank (R² incremental over out-degree ≈ 0; "degree dressed up") and the categoriser/burden/counting gradual semantics (incremental R² over `log(out-degree)` ≤ +0.005 on the norms degree predicts; ρ with degree ≈ 0.6–0.95; and the *sign* is wrong). The honest claim is: **on the `lemma::pos` definition digraph, every principled "centrality / defensibility" score we tried — eigenvector (PageRank, both orientations), discrete (the FVS heuristic's `out+in` key), and gradual (the h-categorizer family) — is a function of degree, because the graph is too homogeneous for the higher-order structure those scores were built to exploit.** That is a *result about the graph*, not a gap to paper over: it says the dictionary digraph has no hidden "soft grounding" signal beyond "how connected is this word", and any graded foundationalness must come from outside the bare digraph (psycholinguistic norms, the typed sense-level rebuild of §7, edge weights from corpus statistics).
2. **The attack-vs-support point is the real methodological note for the argumentation side.** `argumentation.ranking` is built for *attack* graphs; a definition digraph read as an attack graph gives a gradual score that ranks *leaves* high — the opposite of foundationalness. The right import for the dictionary problem is the *bipolar / support* side (`bipolar.py`, `gradual.py`'s Potyka energies, `llm_surface.py`'s QBAF), where definition edges are `supports`; that fixes the sign. (It will not fix the degree-dominance — but that is the homogeneity result of point 1, not an artefact of the wrong library entry point.) This generalises: anyone tempted to "run a ranking semantics over my dependency graph" should check the edge polarity first, or they will silently rank their leaves as their foundations.
3. **The kernel-vs-full and converged-vs-capped distinctions don't matter for the verdict.** The converged kernel-scope numbers and the iteration-capped full-graph numbers agree (the rank order, which is what every comparison uses, is stable by iteration ~25 even when the absolute fixpoint isn't reached). The verdict — "collapses to degree, wrong sign" — is robust to all of those choices, which is itself worth one sentence: it's not a tuning artefact.
