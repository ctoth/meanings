# Adversarial review — "the project is reinventing distributional semantics with extra typed steps and worse data"

**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Reviewer:** `claude -p`, phase 3, assigned angle: the reinventing-distributional-semantics charge (one of the three drawn from `reports/synthesis.md` §7). Read: `reports/synthesis.md`, `reports/synthesis-minimal-core-to-expansion.md`, `reports/swanson-synthesis.md`, `reports/psycholinguistic-regression-findings.md`, `reports/spectral-valuation-oewn.md`, `reports/ranking-valuation-oewn.md`, `reports/sibling-tools-connection.md`, `reports/synthesis-facet-philosophy-{codex,gemini}.md`, `notes/upgoer-identity-clusters.md`, `README.md`. No code read; claims below are about the documents.

---

## The charge, strongest form

Distributional semantics is the thesis that a word's meaning is exhausted by its relational profile — its pattern of co-occurrence / substitutability / connection to other words — with no extra-relational residue. The project's three empirical pillars this round all returned a verdict of that exact shape:

- **Spectral (`spectral-valuation-oewn.md`):** reverse-PageRank ≈ out-degree; a degree-preserving null recovers ~0.6 of the real ranking; "both objects are degree in a thin disguise." I.e. the relational structure already carries whatever "foundationalness" signal there is.
- **Psycholinguistic regression (`psycholinguistic-regression-findings.md`):** the structural block (degree, SCC, cycle participation, PageRank, POS) screens off frequency / AoA / concreteness almost completely (incremental block-2 R²/AUC ∈ [0.0001, 0.01]); the Vincent-Lamarre "deeper = later acquired" effect vanishes once you condition on structure. I.e. the relational structure already encodes the psycholinguistic salience.
- **Ranking semantics (`ranking-valuation-oewn.md`):** every gradual semantics tried collapses to `1/(1+degree)`, often with the wrong sign. Same verdict, third instrument.

Each of these is a *confirmation* of the distributional thesis on this artifact: "the relational structure already encodes the salience" is just "meaning here is relational" said with a graph. Yet `synthesis.md` §4 lists "should not claim … that the psycholinguistic regression proves meaning is relationally complete," the philosophy facets treat "vector soup" as the thing to be defeated, and `notes/upgoer-identity-clusters.md` makes "this is not just an LLM" a load-bearing invariant. So: the project ran three experiments, all of which point toward "meaning is relational," and then frames the relational/distributional view as the adversary. That tension is the first prong.

Second prong — **worse data.** WordNet glosses are a tiny (~160k lemma nodes, ~120k synsets), hand-curated, *learner-facing* artifact. Large corpora and the LLMs trained on them have orders of magnitude more evidence about how words are actually used and what they mean. A research program that builds its entire substrate on the WordNet definition graph — and then layers FVS/Kernel/MinSet/ADF machinery on top — has chosen the smallest, most editorially-biased lexical resource available and called the result "the words you'd need to bootstrap English, done honestly" (`synthesis.md` §0). If the goal is to say something about word meaning, this is the wrong corpus by several orders of magnitude; if the goal is something narrower, the rhetoric doesn't say so.

Third prong — **the lexicographer's confound, taken to its conclusion.** The synthesis (§4, both philosophy facets, the regression report §6.3) identifies the confound: concrete / early / frequent words become definers *because lexicographers expect learners to know them*, which *creates* the structural features. Granted as a caveat about the regression. But push it: if the dictionary graph's topology is largely an image of "what lexicographers believe learners already know," then the graph is **a folk-distributional model of vocabulary acquisition**, not "the structure of meaning." The Kernel is then "the set of words a community of lexicographers couldn't avoid leaning on" — which is a *usage* fact about a *small expert community*, i.e. a thin, biased distributional signal, dressed in feedback-vertex-set clothing. The FVS/Kernel apparatus is, on this reading, sunk cost: a beautiful machine bolted to the wrong measurement.

Fourth prong — **the project already imports the distributional side where it actually needs coverage.** `synthesis-minimal-core-to-expansion.md` makes LGDE 2025 (a cKNN-embedding diffusion method) the "default outward-growth method once a seed has been chosen" and OpenGloss 2025 (LLM-generated) the "future graph substrate." The sense-level rebuild "resolves same-surface gloss references to the intended sense" — by some disambiguation method the synthesis doesn't name; if that method is an embedding or an LLM, the typed graph is *built on* distributional machinery with the typing as an audit veneer. So in practice the project reaches for distributional tools at exactly the points where the typed dictionary graph runs out (growth, generation, sense resolution), while rhetorically holding distributional semantics at arm's length. That is the behavior of a project that hasn't decided what it thinks.

## Where the project's own evidence supports the charge

1. **Three-for-three negative results, all pointing the same way.** Spectral, regression, ranking — every "is there structure beyond degree / beyond the relations?" probe came back "no." The synthesis correctly reports each one. What it doesn't do is connect them: *the cumulative weight of "no extra-relational residue detectable" is exactly the distributional thesis accumulating evidence in the project's own logs.* `synthesis.md` §7 bullet 2 names the charge and the only rebuttal offered is "the win has to come from #3 [the sense-level bipolar demonstrator], and that's not done." That is a concession with a promissory note attached.

2. **§4 already concedes the relational claim in the form that matters.** "Psycholinguistic salience is largely *mirrored* in graph structure on the current OEWN artifact" — that *is* the operative content of "meaning is (here, mostly) relational." The synthesis then treats this as compatible with rejecting "vector soup," but never says how the two coexist. (They can — see the defense section — but the synthesis hasn't done that work; it just asserts both.)

3. **The "convergent rediscovery" finding is self-incriminating.** `swanson-synthesis.md` / `spectral-valuation-oewn.md`: the dominant-eigenvector object the project computes is *the same object* as LSA's leading singular vectors, PageRank, Sraffa's standard commodity, etc. The project plans a history-of-science paper celebrating this convergence. Fine — but it means the project's own valuation layer is, by its own admission, the LSA/PageRank machinery. "Reinventing distributional semantics" is not a hostile gloss here; it's the thesis of the planned paper.

4. **The lexicographer's confound is conceded as fatal-to-the-strong-conclusion but not followed through.** Both philosophy facets and the regression report stop at "we can't distinguish 'meaning is relational' from 'the graph was built from salience.'" Neither asks the obvious next question: *if it's the second, what is the Kernel a measurement of?* The honest answer — "a small expert community's model of learner vocabulary" — is much less than "the non-circular core of meaning," and the synthesis nowhere states the deflated version as a live possibility for the *whole* program (only as a caveat on one regression).

5. **The "not just an LLM" invariants are about provenance, not capability.** `upgoer-identity-clusters.md`'s list (form ≠ sense, correlation is evidence not authority, typed/inspectable/falsifiable) is good hygiene. But every item is a constraint on *how decisions are recorded*, not a claim that the resulting artifact *does* anything an embedding can't. The synthesis (§7 bullet 2) admits this directly: "the burden is on the typed system to *do better* than the soup at something — and the only empirical comparisons so far … returned 'the graph mirrors degree / the norms,' not 'the typed structure beats the embedding.'" So by the project's own statement, the typed system has produced a *more auditable* artifact and not yet a *better* one. The charge's prong (2) — "auditable, not better" — is conceded in the synthesis text.

## Where the project has a real defense

1. **The FVS / Kernel answers a question distributional semantics does not ask, and structurally cannot ask.** An embedding space is undirected and simultaneous: every vector is "defined" by all the others at once; there is no notion of "X must be known before Y," no directed dependency, no cycles, hence no well-foundedness question. "What is the minimal set of words you must fix from outside so that every other word's meaning is reachable by recursive unfolding of definitions?" is a question about a *directed dependency structure*, and the dictionary definition graph is one (a corpus is not — co-occurrence is symmetric-ish and gives you no `must-know-first` arrow). So "the project is reinventing distributional semantics" is *false at the level of the well-foundedness layer*: that layer (Massé/Picard/Vincent-Lamarre, the `meanings` FVS/SCC/leaf-strip toolbox, `gunray`'s `UNDECIDED`) has no distributional analog. Distributional semantics has nothing to say about whether a lexicon is circular, because it has no edges with direction. The synthesis is right to keep this layer central, and the README's framing ("how small is the non-circular core of a dictionary?") is the *defensible* scope statement — narrower than `synthesis.md` §0's "bootstrap English."

2. **Directedness + inspectability is a real difference in kind, even if not in capability.** "Same salience, different representation" undersells it. A directed, provenance-bearing dependency graph supports queries an embedding can't answer at all: *why* is this word foundational (here is the derivation chain), *which* cycles force it in, *what* changes if you ground a different word instead. That is not "worse data" — it is *differently-structured* data that licenses a different class of question. The honest framing the synthesis should adopt: the dictionary graph is not a *better* model of meaning than an embedding; it is a model of a *different object* (the editorial dependency structure of a curated lexicon), and that object has an acyclicity question that is interesting in its own right.

3. **Distributional semantics is the *target* of the symbol-grounding critique, not a rival to it — so treating it as a foil is not incoherent at the philosophical level.** Harnad's "Chinese-Chinese dictionary-go-round" *is* the claim that a purely relational/distributional system is ungrounded; "meaning is distributional" is precisely the position the grounding argument says is insufficient. So when the philosophy facets set "vector soup" against the grounding program, that is a coherent dialectical move: the relational web is the thing whose insufficiency motivates asking where external contact must enter. The charge's "is that coherent?" gets a *yes* here — *at the philosophical level*. (The incoherence is elsewhere: see below.)

4. **"Worse data" partly misfires on scope.** You cannot ask "how circular is the lexicon?" of a corpus or an LLM — the question presupposes definitions, i.e. a curated dictionary. For the project's actual question, the dictionary is not "worse data," it is *the only data*. The mistake is not the choice of WordNet; it is letting the prose drift from "structure of this dictionary" to "structure of English / of meaning."

5. **The sense-level rebuild's self-loop-dissolution result is a genuine, falsifiable, distributional-semantics-irrelevant finding.** "3,413 lemma-level gloss self-loops → 0 after resolving to intended senses" (`synthesis.md` §3) is a concrete prediction that was made in advance (`sibling-tools-connection.md` §7) and came true. It is about parsing/sense-resolution artifacts in a specific resource. Embeddings have nothing to say about it. This is the project doing the thing it's actually good at.

## What the project would have to show to refute the charge

1. **A head-to-head task win of the typed system over an embedding baseline.** Every comparison run this round is *internal* (structure vs. psycholinguistic norms; spectral score vs. degree; ranking score vs. degree). None is typed-system-vs-embedding. The synthesis's own agenda item #3 (the bipolar-AF/ADF demonstrator over the sense-level graph) is gestured at as the place this would happen, but as written #3 is *also* internal (grounded = acyclically-determined vocabulary, stable = candidate groundings) — it shows the argumentation semantics *reproduce* the FVS/Kernel story, not that they beat anything. To refute the charge the project needs: WSD accuracy, or definition-generation quality, or cross-linguistic acquisition-order prediction, or *some* task where (typed sense graph + argumentation semantics) is scored against (a standard embedding / an LLM probe) and wins, or at least ties while being auditable. Until then "auditable, not better" stands by the project's own admission.

2. **A cross-dictionary stability result that breaks the lexicographer's confound.** If the Kernel/Core/MinSet structure is substantially *stable* across dictionaries built under different editorial policies (OEWN vs. Longman LDOCE's controlled defining vocabulary vs. a non-pedagogical dictionary), that is evidence the structure tracks something other than "one community's model of learner vocabulary." If it *isn't* stable — if the Kernel reshuffles with editorial policy — then prong (3) of the charge lands fully: the graph is a folk-distributional artifact and the FVS machinery is measuring editorial habit. This is agenda item #4; it is not done; it is the single highest-value experiment for this charge and the synthesis correctly flags it but doesn't weight it as *the* discriminator it is.

3. **A statement of scope that the rest of the document then respects.** The project would refute the "worse data" prong simply by saying, consistently, "this is a study of the dependency structure of curated dictionaries, not a theory of word meaning; embeddings/LLMs are the right tool for the latter and we use them (LGDE, OpenGloss) where coverage matters." `synthesis.md` §4 almost does this ("the strongest position is modest but durable: dictionary graphs *locate the places where a symbolic lexicon cannot justify itself from within*") — but §0, the README's secondary framing, and the philosophy facets' "vector soup" rhetoric pull back toward the grander claim. Pick the modest one and hold it.

4. **Naming the disambiguation method in the sense-level rebuild.** If "resolve same-surface gloss references to the intended sense" uses an embedding or an LLM, the synthesis must say so and explain why that doesn't make the typed graph a distributional artifact with an audit layer. (The `EvidenceKind`-can't-attach-to-a-decision mechanism in §3 is the intended answer; it needs to be shown actually holding in the rebuild, not just spec'd.)

## Verdict

**The charge is half-right and the synthesis should sharpen the distinction.**

It is half-*right* because: (a) the project's three empirical results this round all confirm "meaning is, on this artifact, largely relational" — that *is* the distributional thesis, and the synthesis frames the distributional view as the enemy while its own logs vote for it; (b) by the synthesis's own admission the typed system has produced a more *auditable* artifact and not yet a *better* one — zero head-to-head wins over an embedding; (c) the lexicographer's confound, taken seriously, makes the dictionary graph plausibly a folk-distributional model of expert intuitions about learner vocabulary, which the synthesis concedes as a regression caveat but never as a live reading of the whole program; (d) the project already reaches for distributional/LLM tools (LGDE, OpenGloss, possibly sense resolution) at exactly the points where the typed graph runs out.

It is half-*wrong* because: the FVS/Kernel/well-foundedness layer asks a question — "is this lexicon circular, and what is the minimal external grounding set?" — that distributional semantics structurally cannot ask, because embeddings have no directed dependency edges. That layer is not reinvented distributional semantics; it is a genuinely different object, and it is the layer the synthesis correctly keeps central. And treating "vector soup" as a *philosophical* foil is coherent: distributional semantics is the very position the symbol-grounding argument targets.

**The sharpening the synthesis needs:** separate two things it currently runs together. (i) The *thesis* "word meaning is largely relational" — which the project's data **supports**, and which the synthesis should stop posturing against; concede it. (ii) The *artifact* "an unaudited, undirected vector blob" — which the project rightly rejects, on grounds of **directedness** (hence the acyclicity question) and **inspectability** (hence provenance, derivations, "why is this word foundational"). The right slogan is not "relational semantics is wrong" but "we agree meaning is largely relational; we disagree that an opaque undirected embedding is the right way to represent the relations, *and* the dictionary instance carries a directed-dependency question that the embedding instance doesn't have." That position is defensible, it is consistent with all three negative results, and it is honest about the fact that — until agenda items #3 and #4 produce a task win and a cross-dictionary stability result — "more auditable" is the only superiority the project has earned. The synthesis should also demote `synthesis.md` §0's "the words you'd need to bootstrap English, done honestly" to the README's narrower "the non-circular core of a dictionary," and state plainly that for word meaning *in general*, large-corpus / LLM methods have the data and the project does not — that is not a weakness to hide, it is the scope boundary that makes the FVS question well-posed.
